# Longform Baby Niche — Content Pipeline Guidelines

## Project Overview
Automated pipeline that generates ~11 minute educational YouTube videos about baby psychology, development, and behaviors. Cartoon illustration style. Curiosity-driven, story-based content targeting parents and expecting parents. Images/videos generated externally via Google NanoBanana (images) and Veo3 (video clips) — this pipeline generates the script, visual prompts, and metadata, then assembles via Remotion.

## Niche
Baby psychology and development — why babies stare, how they learn to talk, what their cries mean, sleep science, brain development milestones, baby body language. Content that makes parents think "I never knew that" about their own child. Curiosity-engineering approach: take something every parent sees daily and reveal the surprising science behind it.

## Target Style
Warm, confident, story-driven educational content. Think: Kurzgesagt's clarity + a science-loving parent's excitement + real research citations. NOT a mommy blog. NOT a medical lecture. NOT clickbait fear-mongering. A storyteller who makes child development fascinating.

### Tier 3: Overused AI Structures (rewrite if found)
- "Whether you're a new parent or a seasoned one..."
- "Not only...but also..."
- "From birth to toddlerhood..."
- "In conclusion..." / "To sum up..."
- Starting sentences with "Importantly," / "Notably," / "Interestingly,"
- "Let's explore..." / "Let's take a look at..."

## 2. SCRIPT STRUCTURE (Adapted from Curiosity Engineering)

### Target Length
- ~1650-1800 words for 11 minutes at conversational pace (~150 words/minute)
- 40-60 scenes (each scene 10-20 seconds of visual content)

### HOOK (0-20 seconds)
Start with a powerful curiosity hook about baby behavior:
- Present something counterintuitive: "Your baby isn't just staring at you. They're running the most sophisticated face-recognition software on the planet."
- Use scale: "In the first year of life, a baby's brain forms 1 million new neural connections every single second."
- Create confusion: "Babies can hear music in the womb... but they're born legally blind."
- Promise: "After this, you'll never look at a baby's smile the same way."

**Bad hooks (NEVER use):**
- "Hey everyone, welcome back"
- "In today's video, we're going to talk about..."
- "Have you ever wondered why babies..."
- "As parents, we all know that..."

### SCALE & CONTEXT
Show how fascinating or surprising the subject is:
- Real-world comparisons: "A baby's brain is twice as active as an adult's"
- Numbers that shock: "Babies lose 100 neural connections every second they don't use"
- Make the viewer feel: "this is way more complex than I thought"

### HISTORICAL BACKSTORY (Brief)
- When did scientists first study this? What did they discover?
- Key breakthrough or researcher (if relevant)
- Keep engaging, not academic. 30-60 seconds max.

### CORE SIMPLIFICATION
- Break the concept into 2-4 simple principles
- Use clean, parent-friendly language
- Avoid medical jargon unless explained simply
- If you must use a term, define it immediately: "habituation — basically, getting bored of something they've already seen"

### DEEP EXPLANATION (But Simple)
- Explain how it actually works step by step
- Use analogies: "Think of it like a filing cabinet..." / "Imagine your brain as a construction site..."
- Use visual language: "Picture this...", "Imagine looking through your baby's eyes..."
- Understandable for any parent, no science background needed

### CONSTANT RE-HOOKS (Every 20-40 Seconds)
Curiosity resets to keep viewers watching:
- "But here's where it gets really interesting..."
- "Now this is the part most parents never hear about..."
- "And that's not even the wildest part..."
- "But wait — there's a twist..."
- "Okay, so here's what nobody tells you..."

### REAL-WORLD APPLICATION
- What can parents actually do with this knowledge?
- Practical, specific tips (not vague "be present" advice)
- "Next time your baby does X, try Y — here's why it works"

### SURPRISING DETAILS
- 2-3 "most people don't know this" moments per video
- Focus on details that feel hidden or overlooked
- Things that make parents say "wait, really?"

### SATISFYING CONCLUSION
- Bring everything together in a simple insight
- Make it feel logical and obvious in hindsight
- Slightly warm/emotional tone (not sappy)
- "So the next time your baby stares at a stranger... they're not being weird. They're being brilliant."

### LOOP TO NEXT VIDEO
End with a curiosity teaser for a related topic:
- "Now here's something even more interesting about how babies learn to talk..."
- "But what happens when babies start dreaming? That's a whole other story..."

## 3. CONTENT & ANALYSIS RULES

### Originality Requirements
Every script MUST include:
- [ ] At least 1 specific research study or scientist name
- [ ] At least 1 prediction or "what scientists think is happening"
- [ ] At least 1 "we don't fully understand yet" honesty moment
- [ ] At least 1 comparison to adult behavior (adults do X, but babies do Y)
- [ ] At least 3 specific numbers, ages, or data points
- [ ] Context a casual parent wouldn't know (research history, failed theories)
- [ ] At least 1 practical takeaway for parents

### What NEVER to Do
- Give medical advice — always say "talk to your pediatrician" for health concerns
- Fear-monger about development delays
- Present one study as settled science
- Be preachy or judgmental about parenting choices
- Use weasel words to avoid taking a position
- State obvious things ("babies need love") — skip to the non-obvious

### Source Attribution
- Cite naturally: "a 2019 study from MIT found that...", "Dr. Andrew Meltzoff's research showed..."
- Don't over-cite — once per major claim
- If the source is a single small study, flag it: "now this was a small study, but..."

## 4. VISUAL RULES — FLAT CARTOON ILLUSTRATION (Reference Video Style)

### Style Requirements (ALL visuals)
Every image prompt MUST match the reference video's exact visual style:
- **Flat cartoon illustration** — NOT richly shaded, NOT 3D, NOT photorealistic
- **Warm beige/cream background (#FFF5E6)** — the dominant color, NOT white, NOT black
- **Clean thin outlines** in warm brown (#5C4033) — consistent line weight
- **Flat color fills** with minimal shading — no gradients, no soft glow effects
- **Simple character design** — minimal facial features, thin linework, cute proportions
- **Muted warm tones** — beige, soft peach, dusty blue, sage green, light brown
- **Lots of negative space** — airy compositions, not crowded
- **Educational annotations** — text labels, arrows, dashed measurement lines, crossed-out words. These ARE used in this style (unlike Fireship)
- NO photorealistic images
- NO stock photos
- NO dark themes or black backgrounds
- NO vibrant/saturated colors — everything is muted and warm
- NO heavy shading, drop shadows, or glow effects

### Educational Annotation Types (use where relevant)
- **Dashed measurement lines** with labels (e.g., "8-12 inches" between baby and parent)
- **Blue directional arrows** showing relationships or interactions
- **Text labels** on white rounded rectangles with dark text
- **Crossed-out words** with red X (e.g., "passive" crossed out to make a point)
- **Comparison diagrams** showing before/after or vs. scenarios
- These annotations are PART of the generated image, not overlaid afterward

### Scene Types (Rotate — never repeat back-to-back)
1. **Establishing scene**: Wide shot of a setting — nursery, living room, park. Warm beige tones, simple furniture outlines, cozy atmosphere.
2. **Baby expression**: Close-up of baby's face showing specific emotion/reaction. Simple features, rosy cheeks, clear expression. Beige background.
3. **Parent-baby interaction**: Parent and baby together — holding, playing, talking, making eye contact. Blue directional arrows showing interaction flow.
4. **Educational diagram**: Flat infographic — brain diagram, timeline, comparison chart, measurement diagram. Labels on white rounded rectangles. Still cartoon style, not clinical.
5. **Detail close-up**: Baby's mouth moving, ears, hands reaching, sound waves, objects. Intimate, focused, beige background.

### Frame Composition
- Resolution: 1920x1080 (16:9 landscape)
- Clean composition — one clear focal point per scene
- Warm beige background in EVERY frame (consistent across all scenes)
- Consistent character design across all scenes (same baby, same parents)
- Target: **60 scenes** per video (matching reference video)
- Average scene duration: **~11 seconds** (with Ken Burns slow zoom)

### What NOT to Do Visually
- No photorealistic or uncanny-valley images
- No dark or moody color schemes
- No busy/cluttered compositions (this style uses negative space heavily)
- No vibrant saturated colors (everything is muted/warm)
- No thick cartoon outlines (use thin, clean lines)
- No 3D rendering or heavy shading
- No medical/clinical imagery
- No scary or distressing imagery

### Thumbnail Design
- 1280x720 resolution
- Same flat cartoon style as video frames
- Large, readable bold text (3-6 words max) in dark color
- Subtitle text in lighter weight below headline
- One clear baby character + one parent/element as focal point
- Warm beige background matching the video
- Red arrow pointing to key element (attention grabber)
- Must be readable at phone-scroll size (160x90px)

## 5. NARRATION & PACING RULES

### Making It Sound Human
- Use contractions always: "it's", "they're", "won't", "can't", "doesn't"
- Write numbers as words for TTS: "one million" not "1,000,000"
- Break long sentences — no sentence longer than 25 words
- Insert "..." for natural pauses before reveals and after questions

### Pacing
- Vary sentence length: short. Medium here. Then a longer one with context.
- Use fragments for impact: "Gone. Just like that."
- Rhetorical questions break monotony — 2-3 per video max
- Pattern interrupt every 60-90 seconds: new angle, tone shift, surprising fact
- After every 2-3 informational chunks, add a "real-world" moment or analogy

### Tone
- Warm but not saccharine
- Confident but not arrogant
- Curious and excited about the science
- Occasionally funny (gentle humor, not sarcastic)
- Talk TO the viewer: "you" and "your" — "your baby", "you've probably noticed"

## 6. TECHNICAL DETAILS

### Directory Structure
```
longform/
  CLAUDE.md              # This file
  longform_config.py     # Pipeline configuration
  run_longform_agents.py # Agent-callable entry points
  __init__.py
  output/
    topic_candidates.json  # 5 topic ideas from researcher
    topic.json             # Selected topic
    script.json            # Full script with scene breakdown
    scene_prompts.json     # NanoBanana + Veo3 prompts per scene
    metadata.json          # Title, description, tags, thumbnail
    images/                # User places generated PNGs here (scene_001.png, etc.)
    videos/                # User places generated MP4s here (scene_005.mp4, etc.)
  remotion/
    src/                   # Remotion React components
    package.json
```

### Asset Naming Convention
- Images: `scene_001.png`, `scene_002.png`, ... (zero-padded 3 digits)
- Videos: `scene_001.mp4`, `scene_005.mp4`, ... (only for scenes with visual_type "video")
- Thumbnail: `thumbnail.png`

## 7. QUALITY CHECKLIST

### Script Quality
- [ ] No banned phrases from the block list
- [ ] Hook in the first sentence — no preamble
- [ ] At least 1 research citation
- [ ] At least 1 honest uncertainty moment
- [ ] At least 3 specific numbers/data points
- [ ] At least 1 practical parent takeaway
- [ ] No sentence longer than 25 words
- [ ] Contractions used throughout
- [ ] Re-hooks every 20-40 seconds
- [ ] Reads conversationally when spoken aloud
- [ ] 1650-1800 words total

### Visual Prompt Quality
- [ ] Every prompt includes "cartoon illustration" style descriptors
- [ ] Warm pastel colors specified in every prompt
- [ ] No two consecutive scenes use the same scene category
- [ ] Character consistency maintained (same baby/parent design)
- [ ] No photorealistic or dark-themed prompts
- [ ] Each prompt is specific enough to generate a clear image

### Pacing Quality
- [ ] Pattern interrupt every 60-90 seconds
- [ ] Re-hook every 20-40 seconds
- [ ] Varied sentence lengths throughout
- [ ] Satisfying conclusion that loops to next video
- [ ] No generic outro
